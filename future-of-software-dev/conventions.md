# Bundle conventions

Frozen on 2026-09-11, before the first batch rather than after two, because these rules are cheap to change now and expensive after sixty files. Changing one requires an entry in `/log.md` saying what changed and why. This file carries no claims about the world, so its footnotes point at the specification rather than at a fact.

Conformance means what §11 of the specification says it means: the frontmatter parses, `type` is non-empty, and `index.md` and `log.md` follow §8 and §9. A bundle made almost entirely of predictions can be fully conformant, so conformance is not the guarantee this bundle's readers need. The shard layout below is.[^okf-spec]

## Layout

| Path | Holds | Type |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `companies/` | Organisations that build, sell or standardise software-delivery tooling | `Company` |
| `roles/` | Job functions in software delivery, each carrying a past/present/future axis | `Role` |
| `sdlc/` | Lifecycle phases, ordered | `Phase` |
| `practices/` | Methodologies that span several phases | `Practice` |
| `evidence/` | Measured findings with a stated method | `Study` |
| `forecasts/` | Attributed predictions with a written falsifier | `Forecast` |
| `people/` | Named practitioners, researchers and executives | `Person` |
| `technologies/` | Tools, protocols and standards | `Technology` |
| `timeline/` | Derived chronological views over the other concepts | `Timeline` |
| `images/` | Media cited by concepts | non-concept |
| `references/captures/` | Raw retrieval dumps, stored as `.txt` | non-concept |
| `_tools/` | Linter and index generator | non-concept |

One entity or idea per file. File names are lowercase-hyphenated. `index.md` and `log.md` are reserved by the specification, in the bundle root and in subdirectories; subdirectory indexes carry no frontmatter and are generated by `_tools/build-index.py`, so editing one by hand loses the edit.

Retrieval dumps are stored as `.txt` deliberately. The conformance checker enumerates `*.md`, so a capture cannot be mistaken for a concept, and a capture is an artefact rather than knowledge: it is the thing a source's `title` and URL describe, kept so a claim can be re-checked without refetching a page that may have changed or gone.

## The split that matters: evidence and forecast

This bundle's subject is unsettled, so one mechanism for attribution will not carry it. Every sentence belongs to one of two epistemics, and the shard it lives in says which.

* A `Study` in `evidence/` states a measured result. It carries the method, the sample, the effect and its interval, and it is falsifiable by being rerun.
* A `Forecast` in `forecasts/` states an asserted result. It carries who said it, when, on what basis, and what would show them wrong. It is true only in the sense that the person said it.

Three rules follow, and the third is the one that keeps the bundle honest:

1. No forecast is written as a fact-shaped sentence. A forecast states its claim in reported speech with the forecaster named and dated.
2. A claim about the future never appears in a `title`, `description` or `## Snapshot` sentence, because those are the fields a skimming reader takes as established.
3. Where two sources disagree in direction, both are recorded and the disagreement becomes an edge, not a footnote. The corpus keeps the conflict rather than resolving it by seniority or recency.

The failure this prevents is specific and already live in this corpus. A vendor-controlled study reports a 55.8% speed-up on one greenfield task; an independent randomised controlled trial reports a 19% slowdown across 246 tasks in mature repositories. Both are published. Both have method sections. Storing them in one shape, with one set of fields, discards the only information a reader needs in order to weigh them.

## Frontmatter keys

Required on every concept: `type`, `title`, `description`, `resource`, `tags`, `aliases`, `status`, `generated`, `stale_after`, `sources`. Optional: `relations`, `image`.

| Shard | Keys beyond the required set |
| --- | --- |
| `companies/` | `founded`, `hq`, `parent` |
| `roles/` | `automation_pressure`, `residual_human_work` |
| `sdlc/` | `order`, `primary_artifact` |
| `practices/` | `era`, `spans` |
| `evidence/` | `method`, `n`, `population`, `effect`, `effect_ci`, `measured_at`, `pre_registered` |
| `forecasts/` | `forecaster`, `at`, `basis`, `falsifier` |
| `technologies/` | `first_released`, `produced_by` |

* `effect` and `effect_ci` are strings rather than numbers so that they can carry a direction and a unit: `"+19% task time"`, `"+2% to +39%"`. A bare number here reads as an improvement by default, which is how figures get quoted backwards.
* `automation_pressure` takes `low`, `medium` or `high` and describes how much of the role's *measured* work a tool already performs, not how much is claimed for it.
* `falsifier` is prose naming the observation that would show the forecast wrong. A forecast without one is not written at all.
* `status` is `draft` until a batch is signed off, then `stable`. `verified` is deliberately absent: under §5.3 it means machine-confirmed or human-reviewed, and it is added only on sign-off, never by the generator.
* A shard key that no source supports is written as `null` and explained in `## What is not established`. An empty field with a stated reason is a better artefact than a plausible number.

## Section orders

Frozen for the same reason the layout is. `##` headings appear in this order, and an empty section is written with an explicit "not established in this batch" rather than omitted, so a reader can tell an absence from an oversight.

| Shard | Order |
| --- | --- |
| `companies/` | `## Snapshot` - `## Founding and lineage` - `## What the sources show` - `## What it says about the SDLC` - `## Evidence it has produced` - `## Relations` - `## Commercials` - `## What is not established` - `## Disputed and unverified` |
| `roles/` | `## Snapshot` - `## The role before AI` - `## What the sources show now` - `## What is claimed next` - `## What automation does not do` - `## Evidence` - `## Relations` - `## What is not established` |
| `sdlc/` | `## Snapshot` - `## Before automation` - `## What changed` - `## What AI changes now` - `## What remains human` - `## Evidence` - `## Relations` - `## What is not established` |
| `practices/` | `## Snapshot` - `## The problem it solved` - `## Era` - `## What it changed` - `## Where it broke` - `## Evidence` - `## Relations` - `## What is not established` |
| `evidence/` | `## Snapshot` - `## Method` - `## Result` - `## What it does not show` - `## Follow-ups` - `## Relations` - `## What is not established` |
| `forecasts/` | `## Snapshot` - `## The claim` - `## Who says so, and when` - `## Basis` - `## What would falsify it` - `## Relations` - `## What is not established` |

`## What automation does not do` and `## What remains human` are mandatory rather than optional, because they are the sections no vendor blog will ever write and the ones a reader most needs.

## Edges

Edges are typed, live in the `relations` frontmatter block as `{ type, target, ... }`, and use bundle-absolute paths per §6.1. Extra keys carry the qualifier: `since`, `until`, `role`, `degree`, `direction`, `as_of`, `note`.

| Type | Direction | Mirror |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `founded-by` | company to person | none |
| `founded` | person to company | none |
| `employs` | company to person | none |
| `previously-at` | person to company | none |
| `owns` | parent to subsidiary | `subsidiary-of` |
| `subsidiary-of` | subsidiary to parent | `owns` |
| `produced` | company to technology | `produced-by` |
| `produced-by` | technology to company | `produced` |
| `uses` | company to technology | none |
| `practises` | company to practice | `practised-by` |
| `practised-by` | practice to company | `practises` |
| `automates` | technology to role | `automated-by` |
| `automated-by` | role to technology | `automates` |
| `changes` | technology to phase | `changed-by` |
| `changed-by` | phase to technology | `changes` |
| `measures` | study to role, phase, practice or technology | `measured-by` |
| `measured-by` | target to study | `measures` |
| `contradicts` | study to study, or study to company | `contradicted-by` |
| `contradicted-by` | company to study | `contradicts` |
| `supersedes` | study to study | `superseded-by` |
| `superseded-by` | study to study | `supersedes` |
| `claims` | forecast to role, phase or practice | `claimed-by` |
| `claimed-by` | target to forecast | `claims` |
| `related-to` | free | none |

`contradicts` is the load-bearing edge of this bundle. It is written only where one source states a result that conflicts in direction with another source's result on a comparable question, and it carries a `note` naming the difference in setting, sample or interest that a reader should weigh. It is not used for disagreement about interpretation, which is what `## Disputed and unverified` is for.

Both sides of a mirrored pair are written. The checker reports a mirror whose counterpart is missing wherever both files exist, because a one-sided mirror is how a graph quietly becomes asymmetric.

## Attribution

Every load-bearing sentence carries a footnote written as `[^<id>]`, where the id is the `id` of a `sources` entry in the same file. Two mechanical rules, both enforced by the linter:

1. Every inline reference needs a matching definition line, in the form `[^<id>]: Author, "Title", URL`, at the foot of the file, and every definition needs a reference.
2. Every `sources[].id` needs to be cited, or it is dead weight and is removed.

The author namespace carries the signal that a credibility score would otherwise carry: `human:<slug>` for a person, `org:<slug>` for a company, press outlet or analyst tracker, `wiki:<lang>` for a community-edited reference work, `team:<slug>` for a named internal group. No credibility field is stored, because §5.1 requires credibility to be inferred rather than persisted. Two further conventions apply here:

* A source that was read only as a headline says so in its `title` and in its footnote definition, and no sentence rests on its content.
* A vendor is named as the author of its own research, so a reader counting sources can see how many of them are first-party.

## Staleness

| Fact type | Window | Rationale |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Vendor product claims, editions and pricing | 90 days | The product surface moves fastest, and it is where a forecast goes stale first |
| Company ownership, parent and acquisitions | 180 days | Corporate structure is slower than product and faster than practice |
| Measured findings and benchmark audits | 365 days | The result stands, but the tool measured does not |
| Role definitions and residual human work | 365 days | A role's boundary moves with the tools |
| Practice eras and methodology lineage | 730 days | Retrospective once it has happened |

## Never written

* **Secrets.** No API keys, tokens, passwords or connection strings, in any field, ever.
* **A number without its method.** Where a source reports an effect without a sample, an interval or a setting, the file says so rather than presenting the number bare. This rule is what stops a vendor percentage from reading like a finding.
* **A forecast as fact.** Stated above and repeated here, because it is the rule a future batch is most likely to break.
* **Rumour as an edge.** A single-source or disputed claim goes in `## Disputed and unverified`, labelled, and never becomes a `relations` entry.
* **Inferred history.** Where no source states a founding date, an acquisition price or a headcount, the field is left `null` and the file says so.
