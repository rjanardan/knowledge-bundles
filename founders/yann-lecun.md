# Yann LeCun

A French-American computer scientist, one of three 2018 Turing Award laureates for deep learning, chief AI scientist at Meta for a decade, and since late 2025 the founder and executive chair of Advanced Machine Intelligence Labs.[^wiki-lecun]

## Snapshot

* Born 8 July 1960 at Soisy-sous-Montmorency, near Paris.[^wiki-lecun]
* Jacob T. Schwartz Professor of Computer Science at the Courant Institute of Mathematical Sciences at New York University, and a professor in the Center for Neural Science.[^wiki-lecun]
* Turing Award in 2018 with Yoshua Bengio and Geoffrey Hinton, for deep learning.[^wiki-lecun]
* Chief AI scientist at Meta from December 2013 to November 2025, and the first director of Meta AI Research.[^wiki-lecun]
* No connection to OpenAI: he has never held a role there, and his file sits in this bundle's `founders/` directory because of AMI Labs, not because of any OpenAI history.[^wiki-lecun]
* Founder and executive chair of Advanced Machine Intelligence Labs, which is run by chief executive Alex LeBrun.[^wiki-lecun]

## Education

* Diplome d'Ingenieur from ESIEE Paris in 1983 and a PhD in computer science from Universite Pierre et Marie Curie, now Sorbonne University, in 1987, during which he proposed an early form of backpropagation.[^wiki-lecun]
* A postdoctoral year from 1987 supervised by Geoffrey Hinton at the University of Toronto.[^wiki-lecun]

## Career and outcomes

* Joined the Adaptive Systems Research Department at AT&T Bell Laboratories in Holmdel, New Jersey, in 1988.[^wiki-lecun]
* Became the founding director of the NYU Center for Data Science in 2012, and on 9 December 2013 the first director of Meta AI Research in New York, stepping down from the NYU centre directorship in early 2014.[^wiki-lecun]
* Co-founded the International Conference on Learning Representations in 2013 with Yoshua Bengio; it adopted the open post-publication review he had argued for.[^wiki-lecun]
* On 19 November 2025 he confirmed he would leave Meta after ten years to found a company focused on world-model architectures and human-like intelligence; Wikipedia dates the founding of that company, Advanced Machine Intelligence Labs, to December 2025.[^wiki-lecun]
* AMI Labs raised $1.03B in March 2026 at a $3.5B pre-money valuation.[^wiki-lecun]
* Became founding chair of the Technical Research Board of Logical Intelligence in January 2026, a company working on energy-based reasoning systems.[^wiki-lecun]

## Research contributions

* Convolutional networks, built at Bell Labs in the late 1980s and 1990s, the architecture that made practical image recognition work and the ancestor of most deployed vision systems.[^wiki-lecun]
* Energy-based models for supervised and unsupervised learning, feature learning for object recognition, and mobile robotics, the body of work he continued at NYU.[^wiki-lecun]
* The ICLR open-review norm, which changed how the field reviews papers rather than what it publishes.[^wiki-lecun]

## Public positions

* Awarded the Queen Elizabeth Prize for Engineering in 2025 jointly with Yoshua Bengio, Bill Dally and Geoffrey Hinton.[^wiki-lecun]
* Holds honorary doctorates from several institutions, including IPN in Mexico City in 2016, EPFL in 2018 and the Hong Kong University of Science and Technology in 2023.[^wiki-lecun]
* The technical bet behind AMI Labs, as the departure was reported, is world-model architectures rather than autoregressive text prediction alone.[^wiki-lecun]
