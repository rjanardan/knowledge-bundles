# Yuri Milner

A theoretical physicist who left the Lebedev Institute, sold computers in the collapsing Soviet Union, took an MBA at Wharton and built the Russian internet company Mail.Ru before turning to international technology investing through DST Global.[^wiki-milner]

## Snapshot

* Born 11 November 1961 in Moscow, into a Jewish family of Soviet intellectuals; Soviet-born and an Israeli citizen since 1999.[^wiki-milner]
* Graduated from the physics faculty of Lomonosov Moscow State University in 1985 and worked at the Lebedev Physical Institute under Vitaly Ginzburg, later a Nobel laureate.[^wiki-milner]
* Took an MBA at the Wharton School, described in the reference entry read here as the first non-emigre from the Soviet Union to travel to the United States for one; he was Wharton's 2017 commencement speaker.[^wiki-milner]
* Co-founder and chief executive of Mail.Ru after the February 2001 merger that created it, and chairperson of Mail.ru Group until March 2012.[^wiki-milner]
* Founded Digital Sky Technologies in 2005 and DST Global in 2009, the international vehicle this bundle treats as an investor node.[^wiki-milner] [^wiki-dst]
* Moved to Israel in 2005 and to California in 2014; has said he has not been to Russia since the annexation of Crimea in 2014, and renounced his Russian citizenship in August 2022, reportedly in response to the invasion of Ukraine.[^wiki-milner]

## Education

* Physics at Lomonosov Moscow State University, graduating in 1985, followed by theoretical physics at the Lebedev Physical Institute under Ginzburg.[^wiki-milner]
* Wharton MBA; the reference entry states he went after "being disappointed in myself as a physicist".[^wiki-milner]

## Career and outcomes

* Sold grey-market DOS computers in the Soviet Union before the collapse, then spent the first half of the 1990s at the World Bank in Washington as a Russian banking specialist, years he has called his "lost years".[^wiki-milner]
* Moved into Russian finance: chief executive of the brokerage Alliance-Menatep in 1995, then investment roles at Menatep Bank from 1996 until early 1998.[^wiki-milner]
* In 1999, after reading a Morgan Stanley analyst's review of online business, he built NetBridge with Gregory Finger and New Century Holding, which invested 4.5 million dollars on condition that Milner and Finger each put in 750,000 dollars personally. NetBridge reproduced American models in Russian: a portal, an auction site, free hosting and an online shop.[^wiki-milner]
* In February 2001 NetBridge merged with Port.ru, the owner of Mail.ru, and Milner became chief executive of the combined company.[^wiki-milner]
* Digital Sky Technologies followed in 2005, with him as chairperson from 2006; Alisher Usmanov became a shareholder in 2008, and in September 2010 the company was renamed Mail.ru Group ahead of an initial public offering in London that valued it at 5.6 billion dollars.[^wiki-milner]
* He stood down as chairperson and left the board of Mail.ru Group in March 2012, with Dmitry Grishin taking the chair.[^wiki-milner]
